## What patina is

An MIT-licensed "pattern-based humanizer" for Korean, English, Chinese and Japanese: a Claude Code skill, a Node CLI, a hosted paid API, a portable prompt and three reviewer sub-agent prompts. About 1,200 files. English has 38 patterns, an 88-entry AI-word lexicon with measured lift per word, stylometry (sentence-length variation, vocabulary diversity), meaning "anchors" checked after rewrite, and a naturalness reviewer with an over-editing check. Korean is the reference language; the orchestrating `SKILL.md` is written in Korean. Single maintainer, heavy churn.

## Findings

### Blocker

None found.

### Major

1. **Its own studies show pattern-stripping has a low ceiling in English.** "H2b (AI-call rate halved): FAILS in both arms". AI-call rate fell only from 92% to 65% (n=21, LLM judges, not humans). *(`docs/research/2026-rewrite-efficacy-study1.md`, RQ2)*
2. **Rewrites converge on a patina fingerprint.** Rewrites are "measurably more alike than independently-authored human texts"; rewriting human English nudged it toward AI (borderline). *(`docs/research/...study1.md`, RQ4 and RQ5b)*
3. **Voice guidance contradicts the Fixed idiom rule.** "Use idiom naturally — but not the AI-overused ones." Daikenja bans idioms above a substitution floor. *(`core/voice.md`, English section)*
4. **Sentence-length engineering contradicts the reader block.** Forces 5-8 and 20+ token sentences per paragraph to reach "CV ≥ 0.35". Daikenja: one idea per sentence, under 20 words. Burstiness serves detectors, not non-native readers. *(`SKILL.md` 5a, step 4)*
5. **Chatbot-artifact pattern would flag normal workplace closings.** Watch words include "Let me know if you need", "Feel free to ask". The exclusion covers chat transcripts and chatbot UI only. *(`patterns/en-communication.md`, #19)*

### Minor

6. **Persona allow-lists add confidence words.** `allow: honestly, so, maybe`. Inserting them changes stance, which `rewrite-rules.md` forbids. *(`personas/en/blog-essay.md`)*
7. **Lexicon flags plain work verbs.** Strict list includes "align", "prioritize", "meaningful", which have no more natural replacement. *(`lexicon/ai-en.md`)*
8. **Lexicon corpus is not workplace text.** Source "HAP-E English paired corpus, GPT-4o 2024-08-06 continuations". One model, essay continuations. *(`lexicon/ai-en.md` frontmatter)*
9. **Patterns were never corpus-validated.** `status: needs-quarterly-refresh`, `last_validated: null`; only the lexicon carries measurements. *(`patterns/en-communication.md` frontmatter)*
10. **Scores are not stable.** "LLM non-determinism means the same text may score differently across runs." *(`core/scoring.md` §8)*
11. **Stale count.** "the 28-pattern catalog" (now 38). *(`lexicon/ai-en.md` intro)*
12. **Passing-as-human framing.** "so text reads as if a human wrote it", despite an ethics doc banning "undetectable". *(`.claude-plugin/plugin.json`)*

## Reuse assessment

Forking or integrating is not recommended: product-shaped scope, Korean orchestration, a numeric scoring model that contradicts the critique contract, and voice rules that contradict the Fixed tier. Adapting a few ideas, in Daikenja's own words, is worth it:

| Rank | Idea | Lands in | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Watch / Fires when / Does not fire when format, with count thresholds (3+ AI words in a paragraph, 3+ stacked hedges), for about 8 patterns: sycophantic opener (#21), throat-clearing (#35), faux-insight (#36), filler (#22), stacked hedges (#23), arbitrary triplets (#10), false nuance (#29), cadence stack (#38) | `docs/reviewer-personas.md` § AI-tell check | adapt |
| 2 | Over-editing guard: a span the rewrite changed that carried no flagged problem is itself a finding | `compose` Step 3 or `rewrite-rules.md`, exempting tone and clarity edits | adapt |
| 3 | Meaning-drift vocabulary: hedged claim made firm, number loses precision, cause becomes correlation | preflight adjudication | adapt |
| 4 | False-positive examples: normal formal register, policy text, release notes are not tells | `docs/reviewer-personas.md` § AI-tell check | adapt |
| 5 | Lexicon method (lift ratio plus a drop list of phrases that also fire on human text), not the list | `docs/voice.md` frequency-count method | inspiration |

Copied wording, if any, carries the MIT notice and credit to `devswha/patina`.

## Questions raised

- Does any candidate pattern fire on the 64,000-word workplace sample behind `voice.md`? Changes which patterns in rank 1 are safe. **Default:** test before adopting.
- Does the over-editing guard collide with `compose` Step 3, which rewrites hostile or blunt spans that carry no AI tell? **Answered from the references:** yes, so the guard must exempt Step 3's tone and clarity edits.
- What licences cover `blader/humanizer` and the Wikipedia-derived catalogue patina credits? Changes how much text could be copied. **Default:** copy no text; restate in our words.
- Are patina's false-positive rates on non-native writers known? Changes trust in AI-tell findings on L2 drafts. **Default:** unknown; the repo never mentions L2 writers.

## Terms

- **MPS** — Meaning Preservation Score, patina's 0-100 fidelity metric. [subject, `core/scoring.md`]
- **burstiness CV** — variation of sentence length in a paragraph. [subject, `core/stylometry.md`]
- **MATTR** — moving-average type-token ratio, a vocabulary-diversity measure. [subject]
- **HAP-E** — corpus the English lexicon was mined from. **unresolved** beyond its source label.
- **SOFT FAIL / HARD FAIL** — anchor check outcomes: meaning weakened vs meaning lost (original restored). [subject, `SKILL.md`]
